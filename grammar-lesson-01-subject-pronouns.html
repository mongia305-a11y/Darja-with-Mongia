01 · Start here

Who are we talking about?

One personA groupSimple sentences

A subject pronoun tells us who a sentence is about. I is the speaker, you is the listener, and he, she or they refers to someone we are talking about. We includes the speaker. Learn the words first, then use them in short examples.

One “you”, two situations. Use enti with one person, whether a man or a woman. Use intouma with a group. Intouma and houma can refer to groups of men, women or both.

Pronunciation tip: in a7na, 7 represents the Arabic sound ح. Latin spellings can vary; use the recordings as your pronunciation guide.

03 · Build a sentence

Small words. Real sentences.

Use the pronouns with three forms of one useful word: Tunisian.

A simpler way to say “is”.

In present-tense sentences like these, put the pronoun directly before the nationality word. You don’t add a separate word for am, is or are.

Ask a question: keep the same word order and let your voice rise at the end. Notice that enti stays the same; tounsi or tounsiya matches the person.

A little conversation.
